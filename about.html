About

Madhu is a three-piece Texas band from the Hill Country near Austin, playing original material with a mix of classic covers. The band’s name means “sweet,” and their sound blends an Eastern influence with country blues and Americana for a unique sound with a mystical element, while remaining quintessentially true to their Texas roots.

Pronounced MAH-doo.

The players

Mark MacDonald

Guitars & vocals

Mark MacDonald, founder and leader of Madhu, plays acoustic and electric guitars and sings. He has been playing for over 45 years and has been in numerous bands as well as a solo artist. In 2003 he released his first album, “Just a Little Levity,” distributed by The Orchard. His influences include country blues artists such as Robert Johnson, Blind Willie Johnson and Muddy Waters, as well as classic rock artists from the ’60s and ’70s. His first guitar teacher was Michael Knust, the lead guitarist of Fever Tree, under the tutelage of Dale Mullins in Houston, Texas.

Mark was active in the late ’90s and early 2000s around Santa Cruz, California, playing solo and in groups such as Chef of The Future, with whom he opened for Robert Earl Keen and Joe Ely. In Ben Lomond, California, he ran into the late Bob Brozman and was strongly influenced to study slide and country blues, with a focus on the National resonator guitar. Mark also started his own record label in 2000, and recorded and produced two albums for the great blues musician Robert Lowery.

Chris Slone

Bass

Chris Slone is a fixture of the Jonestown and Lago Vista music scene, best known as the host of the open mic night at the Lucky Rabbit, where he has given local players a stage and a welcoming room. A Chicago-area native, Chris spent years in the Midwest music world, including running a music institute, before making his home in Texas. A seasoned songwriter and performer, he brings decades of stage experience, a deep feel for songs, and a generous spirit to every bandstand he’s on. When he’s not hosting or playing, you’ll find him supporting other musicians in the Hill Country scene.

Shoni Dawn

Drums

Shoni Dawn has played drums with several cover bands in the Austin area, most recently PolyFox, but is excited to work on a project with original music. Keith Moon and Levon Helm are biggest influences, as well female drummers such as Karen Carpenter and Cindy BlackMan. She brings a great sense of rhythm and a sensitive feel, with an easy-listening but strong backbeat that accents the mood of Mark’s original material.
